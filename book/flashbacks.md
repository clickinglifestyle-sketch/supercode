# THE INTERVAL
## Flashbacks — Vesper and Ambrose

*These fragments are scattered across the novel, in italics,
separated from the present narrative. They are assembled out of
order — the reader builds Ambrose the way Vesper did: in pieces,
never the whole picture, always slightly too late.*

---

### FLASHBACK ONE
*Early — they are students. Ambrose brings her to a dig site near Elathon.*

---

*He had wanted her to see it before the excavation team covered it back over. That was how he said it — covered it back over — as though the earth had a right to what was inside it and the dig was a temporary borrowing.*

*The site was a field at the edge of Elathon, an hour's walk from the village along a path that Ambrose had known since he was a child. A professor from the University of Patras had been working it for three summers. Foundations of something, Ambrose said. Old foundations. Maybe older than the Byzantine layer. Maybe older than that.*

*Vesper stood at the edge of the excavation trench and looked down at the exposed stonework. She was already thinking about the ceramic typology she'd read in the site report, already placing the stratigraphy within the regional sequence, already building the argument for what this meant about settlement patterns in the pre-classical Peloponnese.*

*Ambrose dropped into the trench.*

*"You can't just—" she said.*

*"Professor Makris lets me." He crouched and put his hand flat against the stone. Just held it there. Not examining. Just touching.*

*She watched him from the edge.*

*"What are you doing?"*

*"Thinking about who cut this," he said. "What their hands looked like. Whether it was a good day or a bad one."*

*"You can't know that from the stone."*

*"No," he said. "But I can wonder about it standing here. I can't wonder about it from a report."*

*She didn't answer. She had read the report twice on the train from Athens and had found it more useful than standing in a field, and she knew he would consider that the wrong answer.*

*He looked up at her. The late afternoon light was the particular gold of the Peloponnese in September, the kind that made everything look like it was already a memory of itself.*

*"Come down," he said.*

*"I'll get mud on my—"*

*"Vesper. Come down."*

*She came down. He took her hand and pressed it against the stone where his had been. The surface was rough and cool and slightly damp from the soil around it. She felt the imprecision of it — the way it had been cut by someone working without modern tools, the slight irregularities of the edge.*

*"Well?" he said.*

*She thought about the ceramic typology. She thought about settlement patterns.*

*"It's cold," she said.*

*He laughed — not unkindly. "It's three thousand years old. Give it some patience."*

*They stayed there for a while. The excavation team had gone for the day. The field was quiet except for the sound of the wind in the olive trees at the edge of the property — old trees, gnarled and thick, that had been standing long before the professor arrived and would be standing long after.*

*She didn't feel what he felt. She was honest enough with herself to know that. But she felt something — the specific weight of his hand over hers, the stone beneath it, the three thousand years between whoever had cut this and the two of them crouching in the dirt.*

*"I think you could learn to love it," he said. "Being in it. Not just studying it."*

*"I love studying it," she said.*

*"I know." He didn't say: that's not what I meant. But she heard it.*

*They climbed out of the trench as the light was going. Walking back along the path toward Elathon, he kept pointing at things — a formation of rocks that he thought marked an old field boundary, a depression in the ground that might have been a well, the specific angle of a hillside that told him where the water would have run in winter. Reading the landscape the way she read documents.*

*She walked beside him and took notes in her head and told herself she would write them up later.*

*She never did.*

### FLASHBACK TWO
*Between the early years and the middle — Elathon, September, before she left.*

---

*He was in the upper grove when she found him, moving the way he always moved there — not looking at anything in particular, looking at everything. She had watched him do this before and had never been able to describe it exactly to herself: it was not the attention of someone studying, not the attention of someone searching. It was simply the way he was in a place he knew.*

*She had the letter in her bag. The formal one, on Aristotle University letterhead, offering her the doctoral position in Thessaloniki. She had been meaning to tell him for two weeks.*

*He turned and saw her at the edge of the grove and smiled — the whole-face smile that she had decided, at some point in the last year, was something she would not find if she looked elsewhere.*

*"There's something I want to show you," he said.*

*He walked her to a tree on the western side of the grove — not the oldest, but old, its trunk thick and ridged. He put his hand on it and looked up through its canopy.*

*"Every tree in this section grows east," he said. "The main branches, the whole spread of the canopy — east, toward the morning light. All of them." He nodded upward. "Except this one."*

*She looked. He was right. The tree grew west, against everything around it, its branches reaching in the opposite direction from all the others.*

*"Why?" she said.*

*"I don't know. It's been like this as long as I can remember. My father used to say it had its own opinion." He was quiet for a moment. "I keep coming back to look at it. I don't know what I'm waiting for."*

*She should tell him about the letter. This was the moment — the grove, the light at this angle, him beside her. She had rehearsed it. I've been offered a position in Thessaloniki. It's the right move. You understand that.*

*"You'll come back," he said. Not a question — he had read whatever was on her face. "When you've done what you need to do. You'll come back."*

*"Of course," she said.*

*He looked at the tree that grew west.*

*"This won't go anywhere," he said. "It'll be here."*

*They stood for a while in the afternoon light, looking up through the branches of a tree that had decided, for reasons nobody understood, to grow in the wrong direction. She stored the moment carefully — the quality of the light, the smell of the soil, his shoulder against hers — and told herself she would come back for more of it.*

*She came back less than she intended. She always had something almost finished, something that required one more month.*

*He kept checking on the tree. He documented it in the survey. Anomalous growth orientation, western section, age approximate. The photograph is in the archive in Patras.*

### FLASHBACK THREE
*Middle years — a phone call. She is in Athens. He is in Elathon.*

---

*"When are you coming back?" he said.*

*It was late. She was at her desk in the apartment near the university, papers spread around her laptop, a cold coffee she had forgotten to drink. Outside the window Athens was doing what Athens did at this hour — loud and warm and indifferent.*

*"I'm in the middle of the second volume," she said.*

*"You were in the middle of the first volume when I asked last year."*

*"Yes, well. The work takes the time it takes."*

*A pause. She could hear him breathing. She knew the room he was in — the kitchen of his parents' old house, which he'd taken over when they moved to Kalamata. She had sat in that kitchen many times. She knew the specific sound of the old refrigerator and the way the window faced the upper olive grove.*

*"There's something happening with the lower grove," he said.*

*"I know. I've been following the reports."*

*"It's different when you see it."*

*"I'm sure it is."*

*Another pause. She had an instinct — she could feel it, a specific pressure in the conversation — that this was the moment. That he was going to say the thing they had both been not saying for three years, maybe five, maybe longer. She held very still.*

*"Professor Makris is retiring," he said instead. "They're looking for someone to take over the Elathon survey.*

*"That's good news for the site."*

*"I thought maybe you'd know someone. From the university."*

*"I can ask around," she said.*

*"Right," he said. "Good. That would be good."*

*They talked for another ten minutes about the survey, about the ceramic record, about a paper he had read that he thought she would find useful. When they hung up she sat for a moment looking at the phone in her hand.*

*Then she pulled her laptop closer and went back to the second volume.*

*She did not ask around. Not because she forgot. Because asking around would have meant thinking clearly about why he had mentioned it, and she was not ready to think clearly about that.*

---

### FLASHBACK FOUR
*Late — a message she didn't answer.*

---

*He had texted her in November. She had seen it on the train between Thessaloniki and Athens, read it twice, and put her phone face-down on the seat beside her.*

*The text said: *I think about that afternoon at the dig sometimes. The stone. I think you felt something you didn't say.*

*She had looked out the window at the landscape going past — the flat agricultural land of central Greece, the grey November sky, the occasional dead stand of trees that she recognized from the satellite map.*

*She had thought about what to write back for forty minutes.*

*Then the train arrived at Athens and she got off and walked to the ministry building for the working group and the text stayed unanswered in her phone.*

*She told herself she would reply when she had the right words.*

*She was still looking for the right words when Eleni called in the spring to tell her about the lower grove.*

*By then there was no one to send them to.*
